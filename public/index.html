The source dataset's own diet labels, re-checked

The demo corpus (Archana's Kitchen, 6,218 English recipes, CC BY 4.0) ships with a diet label per recipe. Re-checking those labels against each recipe's actual ingredients shows why tags can't be trusted for dietary safety.

How it works

  1. Parse the message into hard constraints (diet, allergy, "no X", time) and soft preferences (ingredients, course, cuisine, spicy…). English, Hinglish and Devanagari ingredient names are understood.
  2. Normalise ingredients. Every line in every recipe is mapped to one of 249 canonical ingredients via 2,200+ English, Hinglish and Devanagari aliases (99.98% line coverage). Compound items are expanded: ginger garlic paste → ginger + garlic. Brand-dependent items carry "may contain" risks: ketchup, Maggi tastemaker, compounded hing. The cooking steps are cross-checked too: if the method uses garlic, onion, egg or meat that the ingredient list omits, the recipe is flagged.
  3. Hard-filter in code before any ranking. Diet rules (Jain, sattvic, no-onion-garlic, vegan, lactose-free, gluten-free, nut/egg/soy/sesame/shellfish-free) are evaluated against those ingredients. The language model never decides compliance.
  4. Retrieve over the survivors with BM25 + dense static embeddings (Model2Vec), fused with reciprocal rank fusion. Negations are removed from the search text, because embeddings can't express "no garlic".
  5. Rank by your preferences (ingredients on hand, dish-name match, spicy, creamy, light…), penalise brand-dependent risks, and apply MMR so you get five different dishes instead of five dals. About 20 ms per query.
  6. Adapt near-misses. A recipe ruled out for your diet is offered with validated swaps (ginger → sonth for Jain, paneer → tofu for vegan), and the adapted recipe is re-checked against every constraint.
  7. Explain. Each card lists the checks it passed; you can open any recipe to see the verdict for every ingredient line. When nothing fits, it says so and never relaxes a dietary rule to fill the gap.

Verified by an independent regex checker over the whole corpus: zero recipes that pass no-onion-garlic, Jain or vegan mention a forbidden ingredient.
